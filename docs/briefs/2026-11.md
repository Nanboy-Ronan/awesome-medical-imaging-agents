# November 2026 · Research Brief 01

[Brief archive](README.md) · [20-work comparison](../comparison.md) · [Review protocol](../review-protocol.md) · [Main collection](../../README.md)

**Coverage cutoff: 2026-11-17.** This first issue selects five papers for a connected reading path. It includes older work that supplies useful evaluation controls; it is not a complete November publication list. Findings below are author-reported. We inspected primary sources and selected public repositories, but independently ran none of these systems.

## 1. PathLoop: testing evidence collection across scales

[PathLoop, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19527v1) coordinates whole-slide visual and omics interpreters, checks their evidence against a pathology knowledge base, and can request re-examination. It evaluates biomarker, survival, and subtype tasks on four TCGA cohorts.

**Why read it:** molecular information guides where to look, rather than only joining image features at the final prediction stage. **Limit:** retrospective cohort results do not establish benefit in a pathologist's live workflow. **Our research question:** how much improvement survives when the backbone, omics access, and inspection budget are matched? [Audit record](../comparison.md#pathloop).

## 2. ARIVE: making reports easier to inspect has a cost

[ARIVE, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14018v1) interleaves anatomical visual observations with reasoning before producing a chest X-ray report. Training combines report-derived trajectories and anatomical/clinical rewards. Paired review found more report errors when readers could see the trajectories, while review took longer.

**Why read it:** it evaluates how intermediate evidence affects review, beyond final-report quality. **Limit:** traceability does not establish faithful reasoning. **Our research question:** what is the error-detection benefit per minute of clinician review? [Audit record](../comparison.md#arive).

## 3. Taurus: use automation as a serious control

[Taurus, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00247v2) compares aortic CT annotation harnesses with free-form coding agents. Its initial model-review step offered limited benefit; moving useful operations into automation made the refined harness competitive on a smaller held-out set at lower reported cost.

**Why read it:** it separates automation gains from the marginal contribution of language-model review. **Limit:** initial dataset coverage and final held-out evaluation size differ. **Our research question:** what remains after comparing with the same automated pipeline and no model calls? [Audit record](../comparison.md#taurus).

## 4. ABRA: executing tools and finding disease are different tests

[ABRA, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11224v1) tests agents using an OHIF viewer and Orthanc server. Paired annotation tasks replace real perception with an oracle detector, exposing a large gap between successful execution and correct annotation outcomes.

**Why read it:** oracle controls help locate a failure within the workflow. **Limit:** real imaging software running on retrospective scans is not a prospective clinical trial. **Our research question:** does a new controller improve perception, execution, or only one particular final score? [Audit record](../comparison.md#abra).

## 5. MedScope-4B: fewer invented findings can coexist with missed disease

[MedScope-4B, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13006v1) uses lesion-erased and control-edited images to train evidence sensitivity. Internal tests show fewer unsupported findings, but a fresh external test reports zero detections among 48 pneumothorax films.

**Why read it:** the failure prevents interpreting reduced hallucination as improved perception. **Limit:** the method's sensitivity and coverage remain central constraints. **Our research question:** can a system reduce unsupported claims without increasing missed critical findings on unseen data? [Audit record](../comparison.md#medscope-4b).

## What connects these readings

Our interpretation is that these papers motivate three separate measurements: whether the agent acquired relevant evidence, whether it used the evidence correctly, and whether a human could detect a consequential error. A convincing evaluation should also compare against useful automation and match the resources available to the systems being compared. This is a reading-derived research agenda, not a demonstrated field-wide trend.

For a new study, a useful starting design is a backbone-matched comparison, a fixed tool/call budget, an oracle or negative control where appropriate, and separately reported correctness, abstention, cost, and review time. These are proposed design choices; adapt them to the actual task rather than treating them as a universal scoring rubric.
